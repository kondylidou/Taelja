Axiom 1: is_a_theorem(equivalent(equivalent(X,Y),equivalent(equivalent(X,Z),equivalent(Y,Z))))
Axiom 2: is_a_theorem(equivalent(X,equivalent(equivalent(X,equivalent(Y,Z)),equivalent(Z,Y))))
Axiom 3: is_a_theorem(equivalent(X,Y)) /\ is_a_theorem(X) => is_a_theorem(Y)

Lemma 4: is_a_theorem(equivalent(X,equivalent(Y,Z))) /\ is_a_theorem(X) => is_a_theorem(equivalent(Z,Y))
Proof:
  have is_a_theorem(equivalent(X,equivalent(equivalent(X,equivalent(Y,Z)),equivalent(Z,Y))))
    by axiom 2
   and is_a_theorem(X)
    by assumption
  hence is_a_theorem(equivalent(equivalent(X,equivalent(Y,Z)),equivalent(Z,Y)))
    by axiom 3
   and is_a_theorem(equivalent(X,equivalent(Y,Z)))
    by assumption
  hence is_a_theorem(equivalent(Z,Y))
    by axiom 3

    by lemma 4

    by axiom 3
